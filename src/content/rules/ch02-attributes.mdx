## The Eight Attributes

**Charisma · Strength · Luck · Agility · Physique · Will · Inspiration · Education**

Each attribute ranges from 0 upward. The scale:

| Score | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| 0 | This ability does not exist |
| 1 | Far below normal |
| 2 | Below normal |
| 3 | Average — a typical person |
| 4 | Clearly above average |
| 5 | Exceptional — a very strong individual |
| 6 | Human limit |

### Generating Attributes

Roll **2d3** for each attribute, or use **point buy**: you have **32 points** to distribute freely, with a minimum of 2 in any attribute.

### How Attributes Work

When making a check, the relevant attribute is added directly to your skill roll:

> **1d20 + Skill + Attribute**

For example, a Fighting check uses Strength. If your Fighting is 4 and your Strength is 5, you roll 1d20 + 4 + 5.

Attributes generally don't change — except when gained from potions, or through very rare circumstances.

## Spirituality (Mana / Spell Points)

**Starting Spirituality** = Initial Will + Initial Inspiration

Each sequence level you advance adds your **current Inspiration** value to your Spirituality cap.

**Formula:** `(Initial Will + Initial Inspiration) + (number of sequence levels advanced × current Inspiration)`

> **Example:** A character starts with Will 4 and Inspiration 6 (10 base Spirituality). At Sequence 9, a potion boosts Inspiration to 7. Their total is 10 + 7 = 17. At Sequence 8, Inspiration rises to 8. Total becomes 10 + 7 + 8 = 25.

*(If using an automated character sheet, it handles this automatically. Just keep your current sequence number updated in the top-left corner.)*

**Digestion Bonus:** For every 5 points of Digestion you have, your Spirituality cap increases by 1 (duplicate potions of the same sequence don't count).

**High-Sequence Multipliers:**
- Sequence 4 (Demigod): Spirituality cap ×2
- Sequence 2 (Angel): Spirituality cap ×2 again (×3 cumulative)
- Sequence 0: ×4 cumulative
- King of Angels stage: +50% of existing Spirituality
- The Old Days: ×3 total multiplier
- The Pillar: ×4 total multiplier

*(Note: At the Pillar level, these values are effectively theoretical — any blow from such a being is catastrophic on a cosmic scale.)*

## Sanity

**Maximum Sanity** = Will + 10

This is also your Sanity cap — you cannot recover Sanity beyond this value (except through Sanity Shields or special abilities).

### Sanity Checks

When you might lose Sanity, roll **1d20 vs. your current Sanity**:
- **≤ your current Sanity:** Success — no loss
- **> your current Sanity:** Failure — take the corresponding Sanity damage

**Consequences of Sanity Loss (in one instance):**
- Lose **3+ points:** Risk of temporary madness
- Lose **6+ points:** Risk of prolonged madness
- Reach **0 Sanity:** Permanent madness

## Hit Points

**Starting HP** = Physique + 10

Each time you advance a Sequence level, add your **current Physique** to your HP maximum.

**Formula example:** A character with Physique 4 starts with 14 HP. After Sequence 9 (Physique rises to 6): 14 + 6 = 20 HP. After Sequence 8 (Physique rises to 7): 20 + 7 = 27 HP.

*(Again, automated character sheets handle this — just keep your current sequence number correct.)*

**Digestion Bonus:** For every 5 points of Digestion, your HP cap increases by 1 (duplicate potions excluded).

### High-Sequence HP Multipliers

Starting at Sequence 4, the nature of a Beyonder's life changes significantly:

- **Sequence 4:** Current HP cap ×2 immediately upon promotion
- Each subsequent advance also doubles the HP *gained at that level* (e.g., Sequence 4 Physique adds ×2 HP, Sequence 2 adds ×3, Sequence 0 adds ×4)
- **Sequence 2:** Total HP cap ×2 again from the Sequence 4 baseline
- **Sequence 0:** ×2 again

### Death and Unconsciousness

- **HP reaches 0:** You fall unconscious. Each round, roll a **Physique check (Difficulty 10)** or die.
- **HP goes negative:** You can survive negative HP equal to your Physique score. If a single hit drops you below that negative threshold instantly, you die immediately.
- **While in negative HP:** If you take further damage, roll a **Physique check (Difficulty 10 + damage × 2)** or die.
- **Stabilization:** A Medicine check as a casting action stabilizes a dying character — no further death checks required.

## Movement

A **Move action** lets you travel a distance in meters equal to **Strength + Agility**.

## Defense

There are three types of defense difficulty, all rolled against with **1d20 + modifier**:

| Defense Type | Formula |
|---|---|
| **Physical Defense** | 10 + Agility + Armor bonus + Dodge skill |
| **Will Defense** | 10 + Will |
| **Physique Defense** | 10 + Physique |

Some abilities grant additional defense types (e.g., "Deflected Defense" — an invisible force redirects incoming attacks). These are noted in the specific ability descriptions.

## Carrying Items

Some Beyonder abilities depend on how many items you're carrying (e.g., a Magician's paper figurines, a Seer's spell materials). The carry system works as follows:

### Step 1 — Theoretical Item Slots

**Strength × 2** = maximum items you can carry based on raw strength (independent of pockets/bags).

### Step 2 — Actual Carrying Capacity

Your clothing and bags determine how many **pockets** you have. You need both the strength and the pocket space:

| Clothing | Item Slots | Pockets |
|---|---|---|
| Poor clothes | 2 | 2 |
| Ordinary clothes | 4 | 2–4 |
| Formal/Performance Tuxedo | 8 | 4–8 (+ 2 hidden optional) |
| Wizard Robe | 12 | 8–12 (+ 4 hidden) |

*Court dress and women's formal wear may have 1 fewer tier of item slots — discuss with your GM.*

### Step 3 — Overloaded Carrying

If you carry more items than your theoretical slot limit, each excess item imposes:
- **Strength: effectively +1 slot used** (you're straining)
- **Agility: −1** (reduced mobility, affects Physical Defense)

### Step 4 — Handheld / Worn Items

Items worn on the body (scabbards, holsters, slings) count only against **theoretical slots**, not pocket count.

### Hidden Bags

Items in a hidden bag are not valid targets for others' search checks by default — except thieves with advanced detection abilities. If you're unconscious and someone searches you, they need a **Difficulty 15 Detection check** and a **full-round action** to find a hidden bag.

## Bags and Backpacks

| Bag Type | Extra Slots | Hidden Pockets | Action to Retrieve |
|---|---|---|---|
| Messenger Bag | +2 | 1 optional | Move action |
| Backpack | +4 | 2 optional | Full-round action |
| Suitcase | +6 | 3 optional | Full-round action |
| Large Suitcase | +8 | 4 optional | Full-round action |

> **Warning:** Fighting while lugging a suitcase (or larger) imposes a **−2 penalty** on all skill and ability checks.

## Reputation and Clothing Requirements

To wear certain clothing without drawing attention, your Reputation skill must meet the threshold:

| Clothing | Minimum Reputation |
|---|---|
| Poor clothes | Untrained–Trained |
| Ordinary clothes | Trained–Proficient |
| Formal/Tuxedo | Proficient–Advanced |
| Wizard Robe | Advanced–Master |

*Exception: Official or military characters with appropriate justification may requisition clothing above their Reputation tier.*

## Upgrading Clothing (Craft Manufacturing)

A character with Craft Manufacturing skill (or a commissioned artisan) can add pockets and slots to existing clothing:

| Craft Level | Difficulty | Slots Added | Hidden Bags |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proficient | 15 | +2 | 1 |
| Advanced | 15 | +3 | 2 |
| Master | 20 | +4 | 3 |
| Erudite | 25 | +5 | 4 |
| Grandmaster | 30 | +6 | 5 |

*Each item of clothing can only be upgraded once. The artisan's tier determines what upgrades you can commission based on your Reputation level.*
