## What Is an Extraordinary Item?

An Extraordinary item is an object that has absorbed **Beyonder Characteristics** and now carries supernatural properties. Most items have both a **positive ability** (a usable power) and a **negative effect** (an inherent danger to the holder).

---

## How Extraordinary Items Are Created

### Method 1 — Accidental Integration

When a Beyonder dies and their characteristics separate, the characteristics naturally drift and merge with nearby physical objects, forming an Extraordinary item spontaneously. Similarly, if a completed potion is poured onto an object rather than consumed, the characteristics bond with that object instead. These items are random in power and often have strong negative effects.

### Method 2 — Craftsman Forging (Best Quality)

Beyonder craftsmen on the **Perfector Path (Sequence 6: Master Artisan)** can deliberately forge Extraordinary items from provided characteristics. These have:
- Controllable, reliable abilities
- Weaker or more manageable negative effects
- Number of abilities = number of characteristics worked into the item

Craftsmen are expensive and rare, mostly found through the Church of Steam or private artisans at sea. Official channels are often monopolized by churches.

### Method 3 — Spiritual Material Items (Temporary)

Spiritual materials can substitute for Beyonder characteristics. These items:
- Have no Beyonder characteristics and yield none upon destruction
- Slowly lose power: halved after 1–2 years; fully inert after another 1–2 years
- Are relatively affordable (~500 pounds for an item equivalent to a Sequence 6 ability)
- Can give a low-sequence Beyonder access to higher-sequence-level abilities — but at greatly reduced effectiveness (roughly equivalent to the Sequence 9 version of a Sequence 6 ability)
- Sold mainly by the Church of Steam; sellers are often church informants

**Spiritual material item tradeoff:** A real Sequence 6 Witch can achieve restraint in one action; the equivalent *Whip of Slowness* takes three successful hits to produce the same result.

## Extraordinary Item Format

Use the following template when creating items:

```
Extraordinary Item Name:
Corresponding Characteristics (Sequence & Path):
Current Holder:
Appearance:
Description:
Positive Abilities:
Negative Effects:
Stats:
Notes:
```

**Example:**

> **Pen of Nightmare** | Sequence 6 (Visionary Path)
> *A black quill pen.*
>
> **Positive:** Manipulate the pen to write a dream scenario in a book. You and others may enter the described dream; multiple targets can be pulled in simultaneously.
>
> **Negative:** Failing the writing check pulls you into a terrifying dream and deals 1/1d6 Sanity damage to everyone inside. Those who go mad in the dream disturb it further — all occupants risk never waking up.

## Custom Extraordinary Items

When designing a custom item, base its abilities directly on the abilities listed in the relevant sequence in the Sequences chapter. This reduces review burden and avoids balance issues. Text descriptions don't need precise mechanical data — just describe how the ability manifests, and use the corresponding sequence's mechanics for resolution.

---

## Randomly Generating an Extraordinary Item (GM Tool)

Roll the following in order:

1. **Path (1d22 or 1d24):** Roll to determine which of the 22 paths (or Outer God / Dual Path if allowed) the item's characteristics come from.
2. **Form (1d3):** 1 = weapon, 2 = carried item, 3 = placed object/implement
3. **Number of positive abilities (1d2)**
4. **Sequence level of each ability:** For a Sequence 7 item, roll 1d3 (1 = Seq 9 ability, 2 = Seq 8 ability, 3 = Seq 7 ability). Then roll against the full list of Beyonder abilities for that sequence.
5. **Is the item Alive? (1d2):** On 2, the item has some form of awareness.
6. **Number of negative effects (1d3)**
7. **Severity of negative effects:**
   - *If 1 effect (roll 1d3):*
     - 1: Possible death or permanent personality distortion under certain conditions
     - 2: Almost certain death or permanent distortion over time
     - 3: Kills or permanently harms people *around* the holder
   - *If 2–3 effects (roll 1d2 per effect):*
     - 1: Negative ability triggers under certain conditions or periodically (may hit allies)
     - 2: As above, plus the ability puts the holder in physical danger

**Note:** If the characteristics have **spiritual pollution** (from a violent death), remove the strongest positive ability from the pool. For a 1-negative item, it can only reach severity tier 2 or 3. For a 2-3-negative item, it can only reach tier 2.

## Irregular Characteristic Assemblages

Some Beyonder creatures — particularly ancient ones — combine unrelated characteristics without following path rules, producing monsters with hybrid, mutated abilities. Objects formed from mixed, non-adjacent characteristics follow the same logic:

- Usually only 1–2 positive abilities
- Must have GM approval
- Spiritual material versions are temporary; characteristic versions are permanent

---

## Using a Same-Path Item as Your Own Sequence

If you use an item that exactly matches your own Sequence and path, you gain no additional benefits — your abilities already fully overlap. However, if you are in a weakened state and cannot use your own abilities effectively, the item may allow you to manifest the relevant abilities at full strength while you recover.

## Sealed Items

**Sealed Items** are Extraordinary items whose negative effects are so dangerous that leaving them uncontained would harm society. They are a subcategory of Extraordinary items — not separate objects, but items classified by threat level.

### Orthodox Church Classification

| Level | Corresponding Sequence | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Level 0** | Sequence 2 and above | Catastrophic threat; managed at the highest church levels |
| **Level 1** | Sequence 4–3 (Demigod) | Extremely dangerous; requires demigod-level containment |
| **Level 2** | Sequence 7–5 (Mid-Sequence) | Significant danger; requires experienced Beyonder teams |
| **Level 3** | Sequence 9–8 (Low Sequence) | Manageable danger; contained by entry-level teams |

**Naming convention:** Level–Number "Name"
> Example: *3-508 "Never-Ending Nightmare"*

Only Level 0 Sealed Items have their numbers shared between churches. All other levels keep their numbers internal to prevent rival churches from reading each other's inventories.

### Storage and Access

Sealed Items are stored at church strongholds — usually behind the **Chanis Gate**, guarded by a long-term watchman (typically a volunteer who has been slowly eroded by years of proximity to the items; their discoloration and abnormal appearance make them immediately recognizable).

Official Beyonders may **apply to borrow a Sealed Item** for a mission by:
1. Having a civilian or captain fill out the application form
2. Stating the item's designation and the purpose of use
3. Showing the form to the Chanis Gate watchman

Items must be returned after use. Failure to return them risks serious penalties.

**Strategic defense value:** A stronghold with multiple Sealed Items is extraordinarily difficult to raid. Defenders can rotate through items, constantly switching which one is active, effectively making one person an army. Even a demigod could be delayed by a well-stocked team working behind a Chanis Gate.

### Designing a Sealed Item (for GMs)

A true Sealed Item should have:
- Strong positive abilities (GMs may allow one extra ability compared to a normal item)
- **Genuinely lethal or permanently distorting negative effects** — holding it for 30 minutes alone should be enough to kill or permanently derange a person; even touching it may cause mental effects
- Negative effects serious enough that rotating holders is required

> If a proposed Sealed Item's negative effects are mild, it isn't a Sealed Item — it's just an Extraordinary item. Don't reverse the classification.
