## Skill Levels

| Level | Bonus |
|---|---|
| Untrained | −4 |
| Trained | +2 |
| Proficient | +4 |
| Advanced | +5 |
| Master | +6 |
| Erudite | +7 |
| Grandmaster | +8 |

**Progression:** Trained → Proficient (+2), → Advanced (+1), → Master (+1), → Erudite (+1), → Grandmaster (+1)

---

## Character Creation — Skill Points

**Dodge** is Trained by default and cannot be selected via Inspiration, but can be made Proficient through Education.

- **Trained skills:** Choose a number of skills equal to your **starting Inspiration**.
- **Proficient (Occupation) skills:** Choose a number of skills equal to your **starting Education**.

> Attributes gained from potions **cannot** be used to gain extra skills at character creation.

### Converting Skill Points

- 1 Proficient slot can be split into **2 Trained** slots.
- 2 Trained slots can be combined into **1 Proficient** slot.

### Stacking Rules

Inspiration and Education skill slots **cannot stack** on the same skill at character creation. You cannot use one Education slot and one Inspiration slot to raise a skill to Advanced from the start.

**Exception — Reputation:** Reputation may be raised to Proficient with 1 Education, then further Advanced with 1 Inspiration, and can be stacked all the way to Grandmaster through this method (reflecting the possibility of a privileged background).

**Exception — Dodge:** Education can bring Dodge to Proficient, but not higher. Inspiration on Dodge is wasted unless the GM allows stacking.

### Initial Skill Cap

By default, no skill should start above **Advanced**. Discuss exceptions with your GM. The extra abilities from potions are usually more than sufficient.

### Exceeding Grandmaster

Skills can surpass Grandmaster through potions or special events. Each level beyond still grants +1, but improvement becomes exponentially harder. The total skill bonus should generally not exceed +10.

## Occupation Skills

Skills made Proficient via Education are called **Occupation Skills** — your character's professional specialty. There is no fixed list; choose skills that reflect your character's background and life history. (A character might have been a merchant in their youth and a pirate in their later years — both inform their occupation skills.)

## Skill List by Attribute

Each skill check uses: **1d20 + Attribute + Skill bonus**

The associated attribute is listed per skill. Even untrained characters can attempt checks if their attribute is high enough.

### Reputation & Wealth

| Reputation Score | Approximate Available Funds |
|---|---|
| 0 | Up to 20 soli, or penniless |
| 2 | Normal — about 5 pounds |
| 4 | Wealthy — about 100 pounds |
| 5 | Very wealthy — a few hundred pounds |
| 6 | Rich merchant — thousands of pounds |
| 7 | Noble — close to 10,000 pounds on hand |
| 8 | Top royalty — tens of thousands of pounds |

> Reputation represents your **personal disposable funds**, not family wealth. A minor noble might have Reputation 2 while their family is enormously rich — they simply can't access that money without asking.

### Luck

Luck is not tied to specific skills — it is universal. The luck mechanics have been revised to emphasize the role of fate.

**Burning Luck (Free Action, once per 24 hours):**
Subtract any amount from your Luck score (up to your full Luck value) and add it to any basic skill check. This does not change the thresholds for critical success or failure. Burned Luck recovers after 24 hours. Luck cannot drop below 0.

*(The Destiny path has unique abilities that expand how Luck can be burned — see that pathway for details.)*

## Changing Skill Attributes

A skill's associated attribute can shift in context. For example, Performance normally uses Charisma (winning approval through presence), but a player might say: *"I want to win his approval through skill alone, not looks."* This shifts it to Inspiration + Performance.

Similarly, some Beyonder abilities explicitly change a skill's attribute (e.g., a Sequence 6 Judge uses Inspiration + Law instead of Education + Law).

Players may request an attribute change if they can justify it in roleplay — subject to GM approval.

## Skill Growth Over Time

Outside of potions, skills improve through guided study or independent research.

**(A) 6–7 days/week of at least 2 hours of non-repetitive, effective instruction:**

| Transition | Time |
|---|---|
| Untrained → Trained | 1 week |
| Trained → Proficient | +1 month |
| Proficient → Advanced | +3 months |
| Advanced → Master | +6 months |
| Master → Erudite | +1 year |
| Erudite → Grandmaster | +5 years |

**(B) 3–5 days/week:**

| Transition | Time |
|---|---|
| Untrained → Trained | 2 weeks |
| Trained → Proficient | +2 months |
| Proficient → Advanced | +5 months |
| Advanced → Master | +8 months |
| Master → Erudite | +1.5 years |
| Erudite → Grandmaster | +7 years |

**(C) 1–2 days/week:**

| Transition | Time |
|---|---|
| Untrained → Trained | 1 month |
| Trained → Proficient | +5 months |
| Proficient → Advanced | +10 months |
| Advanced → Master | +2 years |
| Master → Erudite | +5 years |
| Erudite → Grandmaster | +10 years |

> **Self-study** (no teacher): Double all times above.
> **High Inspiration:** Every 6 points of Inspiration improves your effective study tier by one level (e.g., C-tier effort produces B-tier results). Maximum: halve the A-tier times, minimum one session per week.
> **Daily cap:** Skill growth is limited to 1 instance per day — cramming doesn't accelerate digestion.

## Education Growth (Breaking the Upper Limit)

Unlike other attributes, Education can increase beyond its starting value through learning:

- **Every 5 skills reach Master:** Education +1
- **Every 3 skills reach Erudite:** Education +1
- **Every 2 skills reach Grandmaster:** Education +1

> Skills gained at character creation through starting Inspiration/Education do **not** count toward these thresholds — only skills improved through gameplay and self-study qualify.
