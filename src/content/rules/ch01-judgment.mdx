## The Basic Check

When you need to attempt something uncertain, roll:

> **1d20 + Attribute + Skill**

If the result **equals or exceeds** the difficulty value, you succeed.

---

## Critical Results

| Roll | Result |
|------|--------|
| Natural 20 | **Critical Success** — always succeeds automatically |
| Natural 1 | **Critical Failure** — always fails automatically, with potentially severe consequences |

### Critical Success — Damage Guarantee

A critical success on an **attack** does not deal extra damage. Instead, it guarantees a minimum damage floor:

1. The attack hits automatically.
2. Roll damage as normal.
3. Calculate half the **theoretical maximum** of that damage roll (round up).
4. Your damage is whichever is **higher** — the actual roll, or that half-maximum floor.

**Example:** You roll a critical success. Your damage is 1d8+5, with a theoretical maximum of 13. Half of 13 = 6.5, rounded up to **7**. If your damage roll comes out to 8 or more, use that result. If it comes out to 5, use the floor of 7 instead.

This guarantees you'll always deal at least half of the theoretical maximum on a critical — nothing more.

### Critical Failure

A critical failure results in absolute failure of the action. The GM (KP) determines any additional consequences based on the situation.

## Difficulty Scale

| Difficulty | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| 10 | Something ordinary people can do without much trouble |
| 15 | A moderate challenge — finding an important object, a routine professional task |
| 20 | Genuinely difficult — a gymnastics performance, spotting a hidden stone on a similar-colored beach |
| 25 | Extremely difficult — championship-level feats, things most experts would fail |
| 30 | Nearly impossible — the absolute edge of human capability |
